# /ceo: make the decision easy

The reader manages many agents and may remember nothing about this task. Rewrite the last message or latest work state so they can act without reopening the thread.

## Decide whether to ask

- Ask only when progress needs the reader's authority, preference, or a tradeoff you cannot resolve from their existing instructions.
- Do not reopen settled decisions or escalate routine choices.
- If no decision is needed, give the result, practical impact, and any material limitation in one or two sentences. Stop.

## When a decision is needed

1. Name the project or task and the decision in one sentence.
2. Recommend a specific action and give the strongest reason.
3. Give up to two credible alternatives. State the consequence and the distinguishing cost or risk for each choice.
4. Include a deadline and the consequence of waiting when relevant.
5. End with a supported question widget when available; otherwise ask one direct question with the same choices. Label choices with actions. Put the recommended choice first, mark it recommended, and style it primary if supported.

## Rules

- Use the fewest words that let the reader make an informed decision. Usually two to four short sentences plus choices. Do not fill a template or omit a material fact to meet a length target.
- Include only context needed to understand the stakes or choose. Omit process narration and history unless they change the choice.
- Use concrete outcomes and supported numbers. Label estimates, unknowns, and unverified claims plainly.
- Do not invent alternatives to fill the format. A single proposal needing approval can be an approve-or-decline question.
- If information is missing, identify it and explain whether it could change your recommendation.
- State what happens without a reply when it matters. Silence is not approval for an action requiring permission.
- Make the brief understandable on its own. Link supporting detail; put the facts needed for the decision in the brief.
